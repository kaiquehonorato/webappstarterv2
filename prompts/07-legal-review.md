# 07 · Legal, privacy, store policy and IP review
Model: Opus · effort high; xhigh for children, health data, AI features, or personal data leaving the EEA · fresh session · before the first submission and after big changes
Start: `claude --model opus --effort high`

This produces a technical checklist, the answers for the stores' privacy forms, and drafts. It is not legal advice: have a lawyer (in Ireland, a solicitor who works on data protection and consumer law) review the result before launch.

```text
Read AGENTS.md, SPEC.md, docs/architecture.md and docs/app-stores.md. Review this repository and the app for legal, store policy and compliance risks. The kit assumes a business established in Ireland, so start with EU law and Irish law: the GDPR with the Data Protection Act 2018, supervised by the Data Protection Commission (DPC). If SPEC.md and AGENTS.md record another country for the business, use that country's law instead and say which of the points below change. Then check the App Store and Google Play rules; then the law of every other country SPEC.md lists for users. Don't change code. Produce a report and drafts only. Cite the article, regulation, statutory instrument or store guideline behind each requirement, check store rules and recent EU rules in the current official pages rather than from memory (several changed in 2026), and mark anything you are unsure about as a question for the lawyer.

1. Personal data map (GDPR Art. 30 record of processing): a table of every personal data field with where it is collected (screen, permission, SDK), where it is stored (server, phone, third party), purpose, legal basis (GDPR Art. 6, plus an Art. 9 condition for special categories such as health or biometrics), who can access it, which processors and SDKs receive it and in which country, retention, and how it is deleted. Flag special categories, precise location, contacts, photos, and data about children.
2. Data protection impact assessment (GDPR Art. 35): say whether any processing needs one (the DPC publishes the list of processing that does, such as large-scale location tracking, special categories at scale, or processing children's data in new ways). If one is needed, draft it.
3. Store privacy forms: from the map, draft the answers for Apple's App Privacy section (data types, linked to the user or not, used for tracking or not) and Google Play's Data safety form (collected, shared, optional or required, encrypted in transit, deletion available), including what every SDK collects. Check the privacy manifests (ours and the SDKs') for the APIs that need a declared reason. If anything counts as tracking under Apple's definition, App Tracking Transparency is required first. Where the forms and the code disagree, the code is the truth and it is a finding.
4. Data subject rights (GDPR Arts. 12–22) and store deletion rules: access, correction, deletion, portability and objection work end to end and are answered within one month (Art. 12(3)). Account deletion is offered inside the app (App Review Guideline 5.1.1(v)) and from a web page that works without the app (Google Play's account deletion policy), deletes the data the forms say it deletes, and says what is kept by law and for how long. List the gaps.
5. Consent on the device and marketing: under Ireland's ePrivacy Regulations (S.I. 336/2011, Reg. 5), storing or reading anything on the phone that isn't strictly necessary for the service the person asked for (analytics and advertising identifiers, SDK tracking, non-essential cookies on the pages) needs prior consent, as easy to refuse and withdraw as to give; follow the DPC's guidance on cookies and similar technologies. Marketing by e-mail, SMS or push needs consent or the existing-customer exception (Reg. 13). Every permission's purpose string matches what the app does with it.
6. International transfers (GDPR Chapter V): for each processor and SDK that stores or accesses personal data outside the EEA, the mechanism: an adequacy decision (for the US, only for companies certified under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework), or standard contractual clauses with a transfer impact assessment. Check each provider's data processing agreement (Art. 28).
7. Children: if the app is aimed at or likely to be used by children, check GDPR Art. 8 (in Ireland, parental consent below 16 for consent-based processing, Data Protection Act 2018 s. 31), the DPC's Fundamentals for a Child-Oriented Approach to Data Processing, the Digital Services Act Art. 28 duties if the app is an online platform minors can use (no profiling-based ads to minors, privacy and safety by default), and the stores' age ratings and Google Play's target audience and Families rules. With users in Brazil, also the ECA Digital (Lei 15.211/2025) and the stores' age signals there. If children are clearly out of scope, say why.
8. Security, breach readiness and product rules:
   - SECURITY.md against GDPR Arts. 33 and 34 (72 hours to the DPC; affected people told without undue delay when the risk is high), including how a fix reaches installed apps (forced update).
   - The Cyber Resilience Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/2847): the app is a product with digital elements made available on the EU market. Its reporting duties apply since 11 September 2026 (actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents through ENISA's Single Reporting Platform: early warning within 24 hours, notification within 72 hours, final report). Its main duties apply from 11 December 2027: secure by design and by default, vulnerability handling with a public way to report problems, an SBOM, a stated support period with free security updates, and technical documentation. Say what we already meet, what is missing, and the support period you recommend.
   - The new Product Liability Directive (EU) 2024/2853, for app versions placed on the market from 9 December 2026: software is a product, and a defect caused by a missing security update within our control can make us liable. Note what that means for the minimum supported version and the update policy.
9. Store rules that shape the product: sign-in (Guideline 4.8 when social sign-in is offered), payments (Guideline 3.1 and Google Play's payments policy: digital goods and features through in-app purchase, with country-specific exceptions; physical goods and services through a payment provider; the EU options both stores offer under the Digital Markets Act, only if the app sells digital goods), minimum functionality (4.2), a demo account or demo mode for review (2.1), user-generated content (filtering, reporting, blocking, contact information), over-the-air updates that stay within what was reviewed, and the Digital Services Act trader status both stores require from an EU business, which shows the company's address, phone and e-mail on the listing. Name each rule we touch and how the app meets it.
10. If the app hosts content from users (posts, photos, reviews, messages to others): the Digital Services Act's duties for hosting services (a notice-and-action mechanism, reasons given for removals) and, for online platforms, the further duties unless we are a micro or small enterprise; Coimisiún na Meán is Ireland's Digital Services Coordinator. If users can share videos, check whether the Online Safety Code applies.
11. Intellectual property:
   - Dependency and SDK licenses: list all, native ones included; flag GPL, AGPL, LGPL, unknown, custom or non-commercial ones.
   - Notices: the licenses we use (MIT, Apache-2.0, BSD) require their notices to go with the app we distribute; check the app has an open-source licenses screen generated from the real dependencies.
   - Assets: every font, icon, image, illustration, sound, animation, 3D model and video is in docs/assets-licenses.md with a license that allows commercial use and embedding in an app, and its attribution, if required. Store badges and screenshots follow each store's guidelines.
   - Code: snippets that look copied from third-party projects without attribution or a compatible license.
   - Branding: the app name, icon and listing could be confused with existing brands or apps; suggest a trademark search at the EUIPO (EU trade marks) and the Intellectual Property Office of Ireland; the listing doesn't name other platforms or apps in ways the stores forbid.
   - AI-generated content: which assets and texts are AI-generated, and what the provider's terms allow.
12. Consumer law, if we sell anything to consumers (Consumer Rights Act 2022 and the Consumer Protection Act 2007, enforced by the CCPC):
   - Clear information before purchase: total price with taxes, renewal and cancellation terms for subscriptions, and the business details the E-Commerce Regulations (S.I. 68/2003) require.
   - The 14-day right of withdrawal for distance contracts, and for digital content or services the rule that it ends only when the person expressly agreed to start right away and acknowledged losing it, confirmed on a durable medium.
   - The online withdrawal function for contracts made through the app or the site, required since 19 June 2026 (Directive (EU) 2023/2673).
   - Who the trader is when Apple or Google process the payment, and how their refund rules meet ours, as a question for the lawyer.
   - No misleading claims, fake urgency or hidden costs in the app, the listing or the site.
13. AI features, if any: the AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689). Its transparency duties apply since 2 August 2026 (Art. 50): people are told when they talk to an AI, and AI-generated or manipulated content is marked as such. Its prohibited practices apply since February 2025. Check the provider's terms, and what users are told about AI output.
14. Accessibility: WCAG 2.2 AA gaps in the app and the pages. Whether the European Accessibility Act applies (in Ireland, S.I. 636/2023, in force since 28 June 2025, for e-commerce and other consumer services, with an exemption for microenterprises providing services: fewer than 10 people and turnover or balance sheet of €2 million or less), and if so the accessibility information we must publish. Equal treatment in providing services also falls under the Equal Status Acts.
15. Scraping and third-party APIs: are we within their terms of service and rate limits?
16. Other countries with users (from SPEC.md): what each adds. For example, the UK (UK GDPR and PECR, regulated by the ICO, which matters for Northern Ireland), or Brazil (LGPD with the 3-business-day breach notice to the ANPD, the ECA Digital, the consumer code's 7-day withdrawal, and the Marco Civil's 6-month access log rule for businesses).

Output:
- Risk table: area · issue · severity · evidence (file:line, screen or form) · fix.
- The draft answers for Apple's App Privacy section and Google Play's Data safety form, and the age rating answers, for me to enter after review.
- Drafts, each marked "DRAFT – requires legal review": the privacy notice in each UI language, naming every processor, SDK and transfer; Terms of Use (and terms of sale if we sell); the text of the in-app and web account deletion flows; the consent text for anything on the phone or the pages that needs consent; the data protection contact section; the accessibility statement if the European Accessibility Act applies; the records of processing; the DPIA if one is needed.
- A list of questions to ask the lawyer.
```
