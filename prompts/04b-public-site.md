# 04b · Store pages and public site: privacy, support, account deletion, app links, landing page
Model: Sonnet · effort medium · new session, after Phase 4 · raise to Opus · medium if the landing page reads like every other app page
Start: `claude --model sonnet --effort medium`

Every store app needs a few web pages: both stores ask for a privacy policy URL, the App Store asks for a support URL, and Google Play asks for a web page where people can request deleting their account without reinstalling the app. Links that open the app (universal links on iOS, app links on Android) also need two files on a domain you own. This phase builds those, small and solid. A landing page that sells the app is optional, and Step 1 asks whether you want one before anything is built. Run it before the first submission at the latest.

A public page is not another screen of the app. Nobody is signed in, nothing is cached, people arrive from a phone on a slow connection, and a store reviewer will open these links too.

```text
Read AGENTS.md, SPEC.md, docs/app-stores.md, docs/writing-style.md, .claude/rules/web.md, the visual-direction ADR from Phase 1 and the tokens in packages/ui. This is Phase 4b: build the store pages and, only if I ask for it, the public site. One Issue, one branch, one PR per step, and stop for my approval at the gate. Use the frontend-design skill with the Phase 1 ADR as its brief: this is the same product, not a new look.

Step 1. Decide what gets built. Ask me two questions and wait for the answers before building anything:
- Who is this for: people who don't know us yet and should find the app, or one company's own staff? Don't infer the answer from the stack, the hosting, the store or the fact that the pages need no sign-in. It decides the indexing in Step 7, and it is my decision rather than a technical detail.
- Do we want a landing page that presents the app, or only the required pages? For an internal app the answer is usually only the required pages.
Then propose the list of routes. The required set for every store app: privacy policy, support (how to get help, the contact e-mail, the security contact from SECURITY.md), the account deletion request page, and terms of use if SPEC.md needs them; plus the app link files if the app uses verified links.

Step 2. Build it in apps/site with the app's tokens from packages/ui. No second design system, no extra font family, no new color that isn't a token. Server-rendered or static HTML that works without JavaScript, so the pages load anywhere and a reviewer never sees a spinner.

Step 3. The account deletion request page. It must work for someone who no longer has the app: they prove the account is theirs (sign-in, or a one-time code sent to the account's e-mail or phone), see what will be deleted, what we must keep by law and for how long, and confirm. It calls the same deletion service the app's in-app deletion uses, so the two can't drift; it is rate-limited; it ends with a confirmation of what happens next and when. The page names the app and the developer exactly as the store listing does. The privacy policy links to it. Add both deletion routes to the data inventory in SPEC.md.

Step 4. Privacy policy, terms and support. Privacy policy and terms are routes that will show the drafts Phase 7 produces after a lawyer's review; until then they are placeholders, clearly marked, and the app can't be submitted with them. The support page says how to reach a person and how fast we answer, and carries the business details Irish law requires of an online service: the business name, geographic address, e-mail, and the company registration and VAT numbers where they apply (E-Commerce Regulations, S.I. 68/2003). If the European Accessibility Act applies to the app (it sells to consumers and we are not a microenterprise), add an accessibility statement page saying how the app and pages meet the requirements. These URLs are the ones that go into App Store Connect and Play Console, and the app links to the same pages from its settings screen.

Step 5. App links, if the app opens from links. Serve `/.well-known/apple-app-site-association` and `/.well-known/assetlinks.json` with the real app identifiers and signing certificate fingerprints (the release one from Play App Signing, which I copy from Play Console for you), over HTTPS, as `application/json`, with no redirect. Only the paths the app handles. A test fetches both from the built site. Then show me a link opening the app on an iPhone and an Android phone, and the same link opening the site when the app isn't installed.

Step 6. The landing page, only if I said yes in Step 1. One sentence saying what the app does and for whom, in the words of docs/GLOSSARY.md. One primary action (get the app), repeated at the top and the bottom and nowhere else. Then only the sections that earn their place: what it does (shown with real screenshots of the app, not claimed), who it is for, proof, price, answers to the three questions that stop people installing, and the legal links. Propose the section order and ask me before building.
Copy follows docs/writing-style.md. Rules I will check: never invent a download count, a rating, a review, a customer count, an award, a "#1 app" claim or a company logo; never show a logo we don't have written permission to use; never claim a certification, an audit or a security standard we don't hold. Made-up social proof is a misleading commercial practice under Irish and EU consumer law (Consumer Protection Act 2007), and a borrowed logo is a trademark problem. If we don't have proof yet, leave the proof section out.
Store badges: the official "Download on the App Store" and "Get it on Google Play" artwork, unmodified, at the sizes their guidelines allow, linking to the real listings, and shown only once the app is live in that store.

Step 7. Metadata, sharing and indexing, following my answer in Step 1:
- Both ways, because a link gets pasted into a chat whoever the product is for: a page title under about 60 characters and a description under about 155, both specific; `lang` set correctly and one `h1`; a favicon set; Open Graph and Twitter card tags with a real 1200×630 image we own, generated from the design tokens rather than borrowed.
- Meant to be found: a canonical URL, `sitemap.xml` and `robots.txt` generated rather than hand-written, JSON-LD for the app only if it describes what is really there, and Apple's Smart App Banner meta tag if I want it.
- Internal only: `robots.txt` disallowing everything except the `.well-known` files, a `noindex, nofollow` robots meta tag on every page, no sitemap, no structured data, no search-engine verification tag and no other SEO work. The required pages still work for anyone with the link, because the stores and the deletion rule need them to. Write in the PR that indexing is off on purpose, so nobody turns it on later thinking it was forgotten.
- Never move a site from one of those to the other without asking me first.
Then show me the rendered tags, the robots rule that is in force, and how a link previews in a messaging app.

Step 8. Performance on a cold cache: LCP under 2.5 s on a mid-range phone over 4G, with an empty cache. That means server-rendered HTML; the main content readable with no JavaScript at all; fonts self-hosted, subset, preloaded for the first screen only, with `font-display: swap`; images in AVIF or WebP with explicit width and height, sized for the viewport, everything below the fold lazy; no third-party script in the critical path, and none at all before consent (Step 9). Measure with Lighthouse on a throttled mobile profile and put the numbers in the PR.

Step 9. Consent, analytics and anything third-party, under the GDPR and Ireland's ePrivacy Regulations (S.I. 336/2011; the DPC's guidance on cookies and similar technologies is the reference). Analytics is a question, not a default: ask me whether we want any at all before adding Google Analytics, a tag manager, a heatmap or a session recorder. For an internal app the answer is usually none, and the server's own logs cover the little we need to know. If we do want numbers, prefer analytics that sets no cookies and collects no personal data, so there is nothing to ask consent for. Either way nothing third-party loads before consent: no analytics, no tag manager, no embedded video, no map and no font from someone else's domain. For anything that does need consent, the banner offers reject as plainly as accept, reject actually prevents the loading, and the choice can be changed later from a link in the footer. A pre-ticked box is not consent. List in the PR every third party the pages contact, what it receives and which country it is in, and add each one to the data inventory in SPEC.md.

Step 10. The rest of the public surface: a 404 and a 500 page that look like the product and offer a way back; `/health` left out of any sitemap. Any form (deletion request, contact, waitlist) follows the app's rules, not looser ones: the shared zod schema on the client and again on the server, a rate limit, spam protection that doesn't track people, a success state that says what happens next, and an error that says how to fix the problem. An e-mail address is personal data: add it to the data inventory with its purpose, legal basis and retention.

Step 11. Checks and gate. Run the axe check, the contrast test and the screenshot tests over every page, at 390 and 1280 px, in light and dark. Then give me: screenshots at both sizes, the Lighthouse numbers against the budget, the deletion request flow end to end on a test account (and the record really gone), the app links opening the app on both platforms, the rendered metadata with the indexing choice and the robots rule that proves it, a link preview, the list of third parties and what analytics we ended up with (including none), and, if there is a banner, the consent flow with reject chosen. Update docs/ROADMAP.md and wait for my approval.
```

**Why every store app runs this phase:** a store can reject a submission for a privacy policy link that doesn't load, a support page with no contact, or (on Google Play) a missing web deletion path. These pages are small, but a reviewer opens them, and so does anyone who wants to leave.

**Tips**
- Check the link preview in WhatsApp and iMessage before launch, not after. Most people meet an app through a link someone sent them.
- Don't show prices on the site until they are real, and keep them identical to the in-app prices.
- Internal now, public later is common. When it happens, run Step 1 again instead of flipping the robots tag by hand: the copy, the proof section, the store listing and the consent banner all change once strangers are the audience.
