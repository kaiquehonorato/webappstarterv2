# 08 · Launch: store submission and operate
Model: Sonnet · effort high (a long checklist where details matter); Opus · high after a rejection you don't understand, a failed restore test or a production incident · fresh session on your computer, after Phases 4b, 6 and 7
Start: `claude --model sonnet --effort high`

Gets the app through store review and into people's hands, and makes sure problems are noticed and can be contained. Claude prepares everything; you run or approve each production and store step. Before this session, read `docs/launch-checklist.md` and `docs/app-stores.md`: most of it is account settings and store forms that only you can change.

```text
Read AGENTS.md, SECURITY.md, docs/ROADMAP.md, docs/architecture.md, docs/app-stores.md, docs/launch-checklist.md and the latest security audit and legal review. This is Phase 8: get the app through review and ready for real users, and make sure we notice problems and can recover from them. Don't change production or submit anything yourself. Prepare everything, give me the exact commands or clicks, and wait for my OK on each production and store step. Check the current store requirements in the official pages before relying on any number in this repo.

1. Readiness check. Mark each item ✅, ❌ or N/A, with evidence.
   - Owner checklist: go through docs/launch-checklist.md with me one section at a time. You can't see those settings, so record my answer for each item, and give me the exact clicks for anything still open.
   - Store requirements: the current Xcode and SDK minimum for uploads to App Store Connect and the current target API level for Google Play, met by our builds; if my Google Play account is a new personal one, the 14-day closed test with 12 testers is complete and production access is granted, or the date it will be; the privacy policy, support and account deletion URLs load; the store privacy forms and age ratings from Phase 7 are entered and match the app.
   - Security: every Critical and High finding from the last audit fixed and retested; the pre-release scan of the built app clean; dependency audit clean, or the accepted risks recorded; any secret that was ever exposed has been rotated; production credentials are separate from development and have least privilege; signing keys exist only in the build service or CI secrets, with Play App Signing on; admin accounts use MFA; internal dashboards and preview deploys can't be reached without sign-in; if the product handles payments, health or ID data, or users under 18, the professional review is done and its findings fixed.
   - Data: the backup plan from docs/architecture.md is on and meets the data loss SPEC.md accepts, with files in storage covered too; one restore actually tested into a scratch database (never over production), with the time it took; migrations applied only through the pipeline.
   - Privacy and law: privacy notice and terms published after a lawyer's review; account deletion works in the app and from the web page, and data export works, end to end on a test account; nothing non-essential read or stored on the phone, and no tracking, before consent, and App Tracking Transparency if anything counts as tracking; the data protection contact published (and the DPO, if one is required, notified to the DPC); the records of processing and any DPIA done; the business details on the site; the online withdrawal function and the subscription terms work if we sell to consumers; the accessibility statement published if the European Accessibility Act applies; logs kept no longer than the privacy notice says.
   - Quality on real phones: the release build installed from TestFlight and from the internal testing track on the device list in docs/architecture.md (at least one iPhone and one low-end Android phone): the main use cases done with VoiceOver and with TalkBack, at the largest text size, in dark mode, offline and on a slow connection; the performance budgets measured on the slowest phone; the forced-update screen shown against a test minimum version; upgrade from the previous build keeps data and the session.
   - Store pages from Phase 4b: the launch checklist section for them is ticked, nothing on the pages or in the listing claims a number, a rating or a customer we can't prove, and the indexing rule still matches what I chose.
   - Success signals: each signal in SPEC.md is measured in production, and I can see the numbers without seeing anyone's personal data.
   - Reliability and cost: uptime monitoring and error alerts for the API that reach me; crash and ANR alerts for the app; rate limits on; budget alerts on every paid service, the build service included; domain, DNS and TLS in place; SPF, DKIM and DMARC if we send e-mail.
2. Store listing, in each store language, following docs/writing-style.md and each store's limits: app name and subtitle or short description, full description, keywords on the App Store, category, the support, marketing and privacy URLs, screenshots generated from the real app on the sizes each store asks for (no invented content, no other platform's devices or names), the app icon, Google Play's feature graphic, and "What's new". Review notes for Apple and the "App access" answers for Google: how to reach every feature, a demo account with fake data (credentials entered in the store forms, never in the repo) or the demo mode, and anything a reviewer could misread, such as why a permission is needed.
3. Runbook in docs/runbook.md: deploy and roll back the API; ship a fix release and request an expedited review; pause a phased release or staged rollout; publish or roll back an over-the-air update, if we use them; raise the minimum supported app version; turn a feature off from the server; restore a backup; rotate a secret, the push credentials and a store API key; reset the Android upload key; block an abusive account or IP; answer a store policy notice or a rejection; respond to a data breach (SECURITY.md: the DPC within 72 hours, affected people when the risk is high); report an actively exploited vulnerability or a severe incident under the Cyber Resilience Act (an early warning within 24 hours). Each procedure tried once, or marked untested.
4. Final illustrated manual: follow .claude/skills/use-case-manual/SKILL.md against the release build on iOS and Android, so docs/manual/ shows the real app. Add a short help page for users if SPEC.md asks for one.
5. Keep it testing itself after launch: confirm the weekly security jobs run and Dependabot or Renovate is on. Help me set up recurring reminders, or Claude Code routines that I create with /schedule, for a monthly Claude Security scan of that month's changes, a quarterly restore test, a security audit (prompts/06-security-audit.md) every 3–5 features, and each store's yearly deadline (Google Play's target API level every August, Apple's new Xcode minimum every spring).
6. Launch plan: submit to both stores with a manual release after approval, so the launch happens when we choose; then a phased release on the App Store and a staged rollout on Google Play; what we watch during the first 48 hours (crash-free users, ANR rate, API errors, sign-in failures, store reviews and support messages); the numbers that make us pause the rollout; and what we do then, given that a store build can't be rolled back: pause, turn the feature off from the server if we can, ship a fix with an expedited review, and raise the minimum version if people are at risk.

Gate: the checklist with evidence, the store listing drafts, the review notes, the runbook, and the list of my manual steps. Wait for my go before any submission.
```

After launch, every new version goes through `/store-release <version>` (`.claude/skills/store-release/SKILL.md`), in a fresh session on Sonnet at medium effort.
